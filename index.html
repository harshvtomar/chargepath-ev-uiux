01 · Problem framing

A clearer charging decision

Design hypothesis: an EV driver may struggle to compare compatibility, availability and cost when these details are scattered across screens.

Goal: bring the critical information into one station card and provide a clear reservation summary.

This is an assumed problem for a concept exercise. It has not been validated through interviews.

02 · Assumed persona

Aarav, the student driver

A hypothetical Delhi NCR student who needs a compatible charger before returning from campus. He wants to compare connectors, review an estimate and plan an arrival time.

Key task: reserve a compatible charging slot with a clear understanding of the estimated cost.

The persona is a design assumption, not a research participant.

03 · Design rationale

Make important details visible

  • Display connector and power on each station card so compatibility can be compared before booking.
  • Use text status labels alongside color to distinguish available and full stations.
  • Disable reservation for full stations and explain their status.
  • Show the charging estimate and fee before confirmation.
  • Use one primary action per stage, with a visible return path.
04 · Evaluation plan

Validate with real users next

Proposed task: find a CCS2 station near Dwarka, reserve 20 kWh for a chosen date, and cancel the reservation.

Planned measures: task completion, wrong-connector selection, time to find the cost estimate, and user comments.

Proposed iteration: revise labels and information hierarchy after observing where users hesitate.

No usability sessions were conducted. These are planned evaluation steps, not results.

Scope & contribution

AI-assisted concept created with ChatGPT for a student portfolio. Deliverables include a user flow, low-fidelity wireframes, a responsive HTML/CSS/JavaScript prototype, reusable UI styles and component states, and documented design rationale.

No Figma file, live station data, payment integration, user research, or production launch is claimed. Technical checks are separate from usability research.

Low-fidelity wireframes

Explore → reserve → confirm. These planning wireframes establish the information hierarchy and task sequence.

Explore: compare critical details before choosing a station.

Reserve: review the cost estimate before committing.

Confirm: retain the key details and provide a clear next step.
